Settings

Damage estimate

Estimated power = effect points, times the type effect, times the same-type bonus. It does not know your stats, so use it to compare moves, not as the real damage number.

Moves with two types

Moves like Ice Punch are two types at once (Ice and Fighting). The guides do not say how the game works out their type effect, and your games showed that it is not "both types multiplied". So the helper uses the weaker of the two types by default: then it never promises "very effective" when the game says "normal". You can find the real rule with a quick test in the game, below.

Help

Moving around

  • The section links are at the top of the page, in a navigation landmark. Press D in NVDA browse mode to jump to it.
  • Every section starts with a level 2 heading, and each part inside has a level 3 or 4 heading. Press H, or the numbers 2, 3 and 4, to jump between them.
  • Results are in tables. Press T to jump to a table, then use Control plus Alt plus the arrow keys to read cells.
  • Short summaries are spoken by themselves after you choose an opponent.

How the advice works

  • "Use" is the strongest move whose Manamon does not take extra damage from the opponent. If the strongest move belongs to a Manamon that would take double damage, the helper picks a safer Manamon with a move that is nearly as strong, and tells you why.
  • "Other very effective moves" lists, for each Manamon, its very effective moves and how much damage that Manamon takes.
  • Untick "can fight" for a Manamon that fainted. The advice leaves it out until you tick it again, or press "Everyone is healed".
  • Your party holds up to 6 Manamon, like in the game. Others stay in the transportal list in My team, and the advice does not use them.

How the numbers work

  • Type effect: start at 1, multiply by the chart value for each defender type. Very effective is 2, normal 1, not very effective half, no effect 0. Example: Insect against Air and Magic is 2 times half, so 1, normal.
  • Moves with two types, like Ice Punch: by default only the weaker of the two types counts. Your game showed that multiplying both is wrong. Settings has a quick test to find the real rule.
  • Estimated power: effect points times type effect times the same-type bonus (1.5 by default) times accuracy. A 30 effect point move that is very effective is still only 60, which is weaker than a normal 65 move. A 120 effect point move with 70 percent accuracy counts as 84.
  • The opponent is expected to attack with its own types, so "takes the least damage" is worked out from those types, plus any extra types you tick.
  • Status moves (moves with no effect points) are not ranked. Moves whose power changes, like Sound Wave, are listed separately.
  • Spirit Sight: if a Manamon knows it, its Fighting and Standard moves can hit Ghost types after you use Spirit Sight.
  • Should I learn a new move: the helper tries every swap and compares the average estimated power against the opponents still to come, for that Manamon alone and for your whole party. It also counts how many opponents the Manamon has a very effective move against.

Where the data comes from

    Some Manamon types in the list file are not sure. They are marked "not sure" in the helper. When the game tells you the real types (the Overview says, for example, "Groller is Earth and Magic type"), fix them in the Manamon list section. Your fixes are saved.
